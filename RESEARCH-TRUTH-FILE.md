## 1. Core thesis and historical argument

**Thesis (defensible):** The Great Trigonometrical Survey's innovation was not triangulation or the theodolite — both predate it by centuries — but the **integration of precision measurement into a durable, state-supported geodetic system at continental scale.** From 1802 it combined measured baselines, repeated angular observation, astronomical control, correction calculation, standardized instruments, and a large, differentiated field-and-office labour force into one sustained framework — the Great Arc of the meridian — to which later topographical, revenue, and exploratory surveys could be tied. Its legacy is twofold and inseparable: **scientific** (better knowledge of position and of the Earth's figure) and **political** (the same framework served colonial administration, military movement, and territorial governance).

**Why this is the strongest version:** it survives every evidentiary caution below. It does not claim the GTS mapped all India, invented triangulation, or fathered GPS. It foregrounds *integration + scale + duration + institution* — exactly what the archival record (Phillimore; Survey of India *Historical Records*) documents as new, while Rennell and Company surveys show what already existed.

**The four theme words, mapped:**
- **Innovation** = integration of existing techniques into one continent-scale geodetic system (§2).
- **Impact** = a precise control framework + Himalayan heights derived by computation (§3).
- **Influence** = later Survey of India practice, tied to common trigonometrical control; the Pundit explorations (§4).
- **Change** = uneven regional surveys → unified geodetic backbone → modern GNSS (different technology, same need for a reference frame) (§5).

## 2. Innovation — what was genuinely new vs. adapted

| Element | New, or adapted? | Verdict & source |
|---|---|---|
| Triangulation (baseline + angles → computed sides) | **Adapted.** Centuries old in European geodesy. | The GTS *applied and scaled* it. INST-SEC (Phillimore). Never say "invented." |
| Precision theodolite | **Adapted.** Lambton had one specially made and shipped from Britain. | INST-SEC / museum (Univ. Michigan exhibit). |
| Meridional-arc geodesy (figure of the Earth) | **Adapted aim, new scale/location.** | Everest's arc explicitly engaged the Earth's figure. PRIMARY (Everest 1847). |
| **Integration into one sustained system** (baselines + angles + astronomy + refraction + computation + records) | **NEW in this context.** The *Historical Records* list "Base-Lines," "Horizontal Angles," "Astronomical Observations," "Refraction," "Figure of the Earth," "Computations & Reports" as distinct coordinated components of the Great Arc. | INST-SEC. **This is the core innovation claim.** |
| **Organized computing office** (calculation as an institutional function) | **NEW as an organized office.** Records name a "Geodetic Branch / Computing Office." | INST-SEC. Supports the "discovery at a desk" argument. |
| **Error discipline** (repeat, compare, disclose, re-observe) | **NEW as sustained institutional practice.** Surveyor W. Hodson's principle: discrepancies should be investigated and work repeated, not concealed. | INST-SEC (quoted in *Historical Records*). |
| **Continuity of plan across decades** | **NEW.** Colin Mackenzie (14 Dec 1815): parties "should persevere on one undeviating plan, without interruption." | PRIMARY quotation reproduced in *Historical Records*. Excellent pull-quote. |

**Safe website sentence:** *"GTS precision did not come from one perfect observation. It came from repeated observations, astronomical checks, correction calculations, and the deliberate investigation of disagreement — organised and sustained for decades."* (INST-SEC.)

**Do NOT** state a numerical closure error, baseline length, or angular resolution unless taken directly from Everest 1847's tables.

## 5. Change over time

| Period | Methods | What changed | Source / class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before GTS** (Rennell + Company surveys) | Route, military, coastal, revenue surveys; chains, compasses, sextants, astronomical fixes; regionally produced maps | Substantial but **uneven** coverage; separate presidency establishments, differing method/scale/control | INST-SEC (*Historical Records*); Rennell 1788 (PRIMARY) |
| **GTS era** | Measured baselines, primary triangulation, precise angles, astronomical control, refraction work, organized computation | A higher-order **geodetic framework** meant to unify later survey work | Everest 1847 (PRIMARY); *Historical Records* (INST-SEC) |
| **Later 19th-c. Survey of India** | Topographic, revenue, frontier, exploratory, geodetic work increasingly centralized under a Surveyor General of India | Greater standardization + expansion, uneven, shaped by colonial aims | INST-SEC |
| **Modern geodesy / GNSS** | Satellite positioning, EDM, digital geospatial databases, global reference frames | Faster, globally integrated — still dependent on geodetic reference systems | — |

**The honest through-line:** the *locus and speed* of measurement changed (field → office → satellite); the *need for a shared, corrected reference frame* did not.

## 6. People — evidence-supported roles only

**William Lambton (1753–1823) — Initiation.** Began the southern-India trigonometrical work in 1802; laid the foundation of the meridional-arc program. INST-SEC (*Historical Records*, which dates his foundational period 1800–1815). — *Say:* "began the geodetic arc and triangulation program." *Never:* "Lambton mapped India."

**George Everest (1790–1866) — Refinement & standardization.** With the GTS from 1818–19; Surveyor General **and** GTS Superintendent 1830–43; retired 1843; author of the 1847 *Account* (the key primary source). INST-SEC + PRIMARY. — *Never:* "Everest discovered Mount Everest" (Peak XV was identified after his retirement). *Caution:* do not quote numerical results from the 1847 *Account* until the digitized pages are inspected.

**Andrew Scott Waugh (1810–1878) — Administration & the naming.** Everest's successor; directed the GTS during the Peak XV calculation and announcement; proposed naming Peak XV after Everest. INST-SEC (Wikipedia/records) + PRIMARY (his 1851 report, to be obtained). — The "could not fix one local name, so honored Everest" explanation needs his original communication or RGS proceedings before it is quoted.

**Radhanath Sikdar (1813–1870) — Computation (the hinge).** Mathematical computer in the Survey establishment (commonly "Chief Computer"). **Documented:** Waugh asked him in **December 1850** to revise the procedure for computing snow-peak heights observed over distances > 100 miles and azimuths up to 45° from north. SCHOL-SEC (Smith 1999, via the "Sikdar and the Final Phase of Measuring Peak XV" article). — *Say:* his role was **computational**, part of a collective office process. *Never:* that he personally made the field observations, "discovered Everest alone," or spoke the famous quotation (§10).

**Nain Singh Rawat (1830–1895) — Later reach.** A later Survey of India "Pundit" explorer of Tibet; **not** a member of Lambton's Great Arc. Illustrates institutional continuation of standardized observation into terrain closed to British parties. SCHOL-SEC (RGS). — *Evidence gap:* no primary expedition journal/observation table was inspected; **publish no numerical claims** about his paces, route distances, or method until an RGS/Survey of India/British Library source is obtained.

**Indian survey personnel (collective) — The labour.** The system was structurally dependent on differentiated labour: the *Historical Records* carry dedicated sections on "Indian Survey Personnel," "Lascars & Followers," "Civil Assistants," "Guards & Escorts," "Commissariat," "Posts & Communications." INST-SEC. — *Say:* "a hierarchy of scientific labour"; name specific roles (e.g., instrument-maker Syed Mir Mohsin Husain, where documented). *Never:* invent workforce totals; never flatten to "unsung heroes" without specifics.

## 7. Peak XV / Mount Everest — verified fact vs. mythology

**VERIFIED / usable (with class):**
- The mountain was known in survey records as **"Peak XV."** (INST-SEC)
- The calculation occurred under **Waugh's** GTS administration, after Everest's retirement. (INST-SEC)
- Observations came from stations **~100+ miles** away — long-distance, requiring curvature + refraction treatment. (SCHOL-SEC; confirm distance in a primary table before a calculator uses it.)
- **Sikdar's documented role is computational** (Dec 1850 procedure revision). (SCHOL-SEC, Smith 1999)
- The published 19th-century figure was **29,002 feet.** (INST/SEC — the announcement is ~1856.) Modern surveyed height ≈ **29,032 ft / 8,848.86 m** (independent modern reference).
- Waugh proposed the name; **"Mount Everest" adopted 1865** (commonly via the RGS). (SEC — obtain the original RGS proceedings for exact wording/mechanism.)

**MYTH / DELETE:**
- ❌ "Sikdar said, *'Sir, I have discovered the highest mountain in the world.'*" — no reliable early source. **Do not use.**
- ❌ "Waugh added two feet to 29,000 so it wouldn't look round." — explicitly a **popular legend**; no primary documentation. **Do not present as fact.** (Safe version: *"29,002 ft was published; the story that two feet were added is unverified."*)
- ❌ "Sikdar discovered Everest" / "Everest discovered Everest." — both false as stated.

**RECONSTRUCTION STATUS: NONE.** No single primary table was inspected giving, in one reproducible chain, station names, dates, elevations, distances, vertical angles, and curvature/refraction values → **29,002 ft.** Therefore **any height calculator must be labelled an ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL**, not a reconstruction of the Peak XV computation. (This confirms the site's current illustrative labelling is correct.)
